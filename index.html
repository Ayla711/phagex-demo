Prototype · rule-based detection

Can software notice a scam before you do?

PhageX watches a conversation the way an anti-fraud investigator would: not one message at a time, but as a story. Friendly openers are fine. Affection is fine. Money after affection is the pattern. Play a scripted chat or paste your own, and watch the risk meter and the stage change as the conversation moves.

    How it works

    29 tactic patterns

    Each incoming message is matched against patterns drawn from real cases: the wrong-number opener, “fate”, love-bombing, asking for secrecy, insider tips, guaranteed returns, “borrow from the bank”, fees to withdraw. Every match comes with a plain-language reason.

    Stage, not keywords

    The conversation moves through the stages victims describe — Trapping, Raising, Killing. Money talk weighs 1.5× once affection has appeared, and a money request wrapped in affection or a hardship story adds a bonus. A warning fires only when the stage advances, never on a single warm message.

    Tested on public cases

    On 95 scammer lines from ProPublica, Forbes, NPR and a leaked training manual, 87% receive a tactic tag. On 17 real chat screenshots from the SafeRoute dataset, 10 of 11 scams reach danger and all 4 safe chats stay safe; 20 benign look-alikes produce no false alarm. Rules were tuned on this same data, so the next step is testing on conversations it has never seen.

    What it can't do

    English only. Regular expressions, not a language model — paraphrases slip through, and it has never met a real scammer who adapts. It is a design probe to study how and when a warning should interrupt, not a product.
